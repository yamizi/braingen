About

An anti-benchmark workshop

Virtual Brain Twins (VBTs) combine generative AI with biologically grounded simulation to forecast how a disease will evolve in an individual patient: tumour growth in glioblastoma and low-grade glioma, cognitive and motor decline in Alzheimer's and Parkinson's disease.

The field is organised around one incentive: the best score on a benchmark. BrainGEN inverts that logic. Instead of asking how to build a twin that performs better, we ask when it should not be trusted in the clinic, across oncology and neurodegeneration at once.

No single discipline can answer this, and no conventional funding instrument supports the boundary-crossing collaboration it needs. This workshop is that instrument.

Objectives

What we will deliver

A first shared framework defining the conditions under which Virtual Brain Twins should not be trusted for clinical translation, plus the collaborations and proposals to take it forward.

01

Shared vocabulary

A glossary that lets a mathematician, clinician, epidemiologist, philosopher and legal scholar discuss the same VBT without mistranslation.

02

How twins fail

Joint stress-testing of real cases across validation, fairness, interpretability, EU AI Act compliance and consent for synthetic patient data.

03

Shared or specific?

Are the conditions of distrust common to both pathologies or specific to each? Only answerable with both clinical communities in one room.

04

Paper and roadmap

A position paper and a concrete roadmap for follow-on proposals to IAS AUDACITY, the FNR and Horizon Europe.

Research questions

  • Are the conditions under which a VBT should not be trusted shared across brain oncology and neurodegeneration, or specific to each?
  • What minimum validation, fairness and interpretability standards must a VBT meet before deployment, and how do they map onto EU AI Act high-risk requirements?
  • What governance and consent framework is adequate for synthetic patient data generated by these models?

Format

Case-based confrontation, not panels

The comparison is performed rather than merely discussed. Two real, anonymised cases, a glioma twin and a Parkinson's twin, are run through each failure dimension in parallel.

  1. Weeks −6 to −1

    Shared ground

    Structured evidence briefing, one-page position statements from every participant and a draft glossary circulated for comment.

  2. 3 days · Luxembourg

    Paired stress-testing

    Glossary agreed face to face, then one failure dimension per session using the 1-2-4-All format, tracked on a live shared-vs-specific map.

  3. Within 6 weeks

    Position paper

    Each section drafted by a mixed pair, never a single discipline, with two anonymous review rounds preserving expert caveats.

Experts

The people who know how models fail

Eight women and seven men, from Luxembourg and across Europe and the US, spanning medical imaging AI, neuro-oncology, mathematical oncology, epidemiology, philosophy of AI and health law.

Interdisciplinarity

Constitutive, not added on

Is a Virtual Brain Twin equitable, can clinicians rely on it, and do patients understand it? Technical metrics need epidemiological validation; regulatory judgements demand philosophical interpretation; clinical usability needs physician assessment.

For example, applying missing-data methodology to a tumour-growth model reveals whether its confidence is genuinely reliable or an artefact. The organisers span three University of Luxembourg entities: LCSB, SnT and the Faculty of Humanities, Education and Social Sciences.

Outcomes

A launchpad for what comes next

Position paper

Principles for Responsible Generative AI in Clinical Brain Imaging, the first framework co-authored by AI researchers, clinicians, epidemiologists and legal scholars on VBT trustworthiness. Published open access; targeting high-impact interdisciplinary journals and a top AI conference, with an extended version in a Q1 journal.

Follow-on funding

National: FNR WEAVE / INTER. EU: Horizon Europe Health 2027 calls on Virtual Human Twins for clinical decision support and AGI for healthcare. Plus IAS AUDACITY and industrial / philanthropic challenge grants.

A lasting network

A shared mailing list, a public-facing summary via UL channels and a follow-up virtual session six months after the workshop to track progress toward the proposals.
